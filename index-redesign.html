Robotics Researcher — Carnegie Mellon

George Ortiz

Designing robust control policies for legged robots that can walk quickly through mud. Mud is a non-Newtonian fluid, making it a challenge for the robot to walk through as it experiences a range of forces as it steps in and out of the mud. I model these forces so that a quadruped can adapt its gait in real time to maintain stability and traction.

Who I am

I'm an M.S. student at Carnegie Mellon in the Robomechanics Lab, advised by Aaron Johnson. My research is on robot simulation and reinforcement learning, focused on robust control policies for quadruped locomotion over terrain that deforms underfoot.

Before CMU I earned a B.E. in Mechanical Engineering at Stevens, where I worked with Kishore Pochiraju on manipulator path planning. Outside the lab, I enjoy spending my time woodworking and cooking delicious food.
